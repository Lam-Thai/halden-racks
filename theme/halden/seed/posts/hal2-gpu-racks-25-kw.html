Why density matters

A typical colocation rack is sized for 5 to 8 kW. A single 8-GPU server can draw more than 10 kW on its own under training load. In a low-density hall, that forces you to spread one cluster across half-empty racks. You pay for floor space you cannot use, you run more cables between nodes, and you add latency where your workload is most sensitive to it.

At 25 kW a cabinet, the same cluster sits in fewer racks, closer together, on shorter runs. That is the whole point of HAL2.

Cooled by Alder Lake

Power is only half of density. Every kilowatt you put into a rack comes back out as heat, and heat is what usually caps a hall. 200 Foundry Row is cooled through deep lake water, drawn from Alder Lake through the town's lake-water loop. That is what lets HAL2 hold dense cabinets at full load, all year.
